Instructions.

As instructed by Mr Navas, Con O'Regan Associates Ltd were requested to undertake a
Structural Inspection on the existing two storey semidetached building at the above address.

The purpose of the inspection was to report on the structure and advise on its condition. No
opening up works were undertaken and no other means of assessment other than visual
assessmentwere employed during the course ofthe inspection. The inspection was limited to
the main structure and specifically excluded any fitted furniture and fittings unless specifically
noted within the report. The inspection of the lighting circuits and heating system was limited
to verifying whether these systems were operational only and did not consider performance or
efficiency.

The Building.

The building is a two storey semi-detached building constructed circa 2016, using standard
masonry building materials on the outer leaf and timber frame construction on the inner leaf
with timber trussed rafter roof and floor construction. The building itself is set around an
entrance hall and landings with living rooms and bedrooms arranged to the right hand side of
same. On entering the front entrance a central hall gives accessto a living room and onto the
kitchen and dining area to the rear. A staircase gives accessto the first floor.

The Roof.

The roof over the building is a standard A-frame roof. Timber trussed rafters, generally at
600mm centres, forms the structure to the roof whilst the roof itself is completed with concrete
tiles on battens and felt. A two ply girder truss caries the truss rafters across the front bay
window. These girder üusses are supported on the bay window side wall panels. Water tanks
are accommodated within the roof space in the standard manner.

The water tanks are insulated and covered as required by the Building Regulations. Standard
fruss rafter bracing and binders have been provided throughout. The gable walls are tied back
to the roof structure using the truss braces and binders secured to the spandrel walls using
100mm x 44mm timber straps. The main roof is insulated with 300mm fibre blown rock wool
fibre and ventilated as required by the Building Regulations.

The party wall between the adjoining dwellings is of a timber frame construction finished with
two sheets of plasterboard. This form of construction is permissible under the Building
Regulations. The plasterboard has been finished with tape and filler at all joints and along the
roof finishes to prevent the spread of fire.

A rigid duct connects the extractor ventilators to the ridge tiles.

Discussion.

In generalthe building is in a very good structural condition with no evidenceof significant
structural damage.Both the building fabric and finishes are in a good stateof repair and are
generally free from defects, Two doors at first floor level were catching on the floor and
require local adjustment.

The internal gasboiler needs to be inspected and serviced by a plumber to ensure that the unit
is working correctly.

Local, albeit minor. level ofshrinkage cracks were noted on the door architraves. These minor
deficiencies can be addressed during normal redecoration. The gap between the window
frames and the plaster reveals/window boards should be sealed to minimise heat losses by air
infiltration at these points.
